# Localization & Cultural Adaptation

Localization goes beyond translation: it adapts a message so it lands correctly in the target market's culture, conventions, and expectations.

## What to adapt
- **Idioms & humor:** replace source idioms, puns, and culturally-specific jokes with equivalents that work locally — or rewrite the idea plainly if no equivalent exists.
- **Examples & references:** swap culturally-specific examples (sports, holidays, public figures, food, pop culture) for ones the target audience recognizes.
- **Formats:** currency and symbols, units (metric/imperial), date/time, number separators, address and phone formats, name order (given/family), and honorifics.
- **Tone & directness:** calibrate politeness, formality, and directness to local norms (e.g. high-context vs low-context cultures, T–V distinction, business email etiquette).
- **Imagery & color:** flag visuals, colors, gestures, or symbols that carry different or negative connotations in the target culture.

## Sensitivity & compliance
- Avoid content that is taboo, offensive, or legally restricted in the target market (religion, politics, gender norms, alcohol, gambling, etc.). Flag risks instead of silently shipping them.
- Respect inclusive-language conventions of the target locale.

## Process
1. Identify the exact target locale (language + region — es-MX ≠ es-ES, ar-EG ≠ ar-SA) and the content's purpose.
2. Translate faithfully, then adapt the items above.
3. Keep brand voice and core message intact across locales.
4. Call out anything that needs a human/market-expert decision (legal claims, regulated terms, brand names that mean something local).

## Output
Deliver locale-ready content in the target format, plus a short list of the adaptations you made and any cultural/legal risks the user should review.
